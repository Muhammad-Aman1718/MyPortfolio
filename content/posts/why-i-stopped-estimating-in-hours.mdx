## The problem with hours

Hourly billing puts the client and the engineer on opposite sides of every decision. The client wants fewer hours; I get paid for more. Every question costs them money, so they stop asking — and the questions they don't ask are where projects go wrong.

## What I do instead

1. A paid discovery call that ends with a written scope: what's in, what's out, and what “done” means.
2. A fixed price for that scope, with the risk priced in — by me, not discovered later by the client.
3. Changes are welcome and handled as small, separately priced additions, never as silent scope creep.

## What changed

Clients ask more questions now, because asking is free. Meetings got shorter, because nobody is watching a meter. And I got faster, because finishing early is finally rewarded instead of penalised.

<Callout title="When hours still make sense">
Open-ended work — audits that turn into investigations, or ongoing support — still runs weekly. The rule is simple: fixed price when the outcome can be described, time-based when it can't.
</Callout>
